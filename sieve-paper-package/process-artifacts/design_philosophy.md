# Design Philosophy: "Quiet Instrument"

A visual movement for scientific method figures.

## The Philosophy

Quiet Instrument treats every diagram as a laboratory instrument panel:
calibrated, restrained, and legible at arm's length. Space is the primary
material. Generous white margins act as the bench on which a small number
of precisely machined modules rest. Form follows measurement: every block
is a rounded rectangle whose proportions echo a data card, every arrow a
thin conduit that carries flow direction without decoration. Composition
is a single left-to-right current, interrupted once by a deliberate
vertical drop, so the eye reads the pipeline the way a reader reads a
sentence, subject first, verb second, result last.

Color is used as instrumentation, never as celebration. A pale slate-blue
field carries structure; a muted sage tint marks the parts of the system
that are new; a warm sand tone marks the data that flows through. These
three hues live at low saturation, separated more by lightness than by
chroma, so the figure survives grayscale printing and color-vision
differences alike. Accent appears exactly twice in the whole composition:
once on the selection gate, the conceptual heart of the method, and once
on the measured outcome. Nothing else is allowed to raise its voice.

Rhythm comes from repetition at two scales: small glyph rows inside
modules that read like tick marks on a dial, and a stepped silhouette
across the top of the composition that suggests a signal being sampled.
Negative space between modules is wide and even, a quiet grid that the
inhabitant modules never touch. The overall hierarchy is unmistakable
within one second: one intake, one central gate, one output, three labels
of evidence underneath.

Typography is whispered: a single humanist sans at three calibrated
sizes, all lowercase or small caps, used only to name modules and name
quantities. There are no sentences in the figure, no paragraphs, no
explanations. Letters are instruments of identification, not narration.
Every label sits inside or beside its module with equal padding, set on
the same baseline grid, kerned as if the typesetter had all day.

The finished work should look like it took a hundred iterations of
micro-adjustment: alignment snapped to a hidden 4-unit grid, stroke
weights reduced until further reduction would break print legibility,
corner radii identical across all modules, arrowheads matched in angle
and mass. It is the product of deep expertise in restraint: the craft
that lets a reader understand a method in ten seconds and trust it,
because nothing on the page argues louder than the evidence.

## Applied Brief (Sieve Figure 1)

- Canvas: single page, white background, vector PDF, wide aspect (2:1).
- Reading current: context enters left, sentence grid, the selection
  gate (sage accent) applies relevance, position, redundancy; the kept
  subset exits right into the LLM; a small dial row below shows budget.
- Palette (Okabe-Ito adjacent, desaturated): structure #7A9BB5,
  novelty #8FB7A3, data #D9C08A, ink #2B2F33, paper #FFFFFF.
- Labels in English, lowercase, <= 14 characters, three sizes only.
- No 3D, no shadows, no gradients, no icons that narrate.
